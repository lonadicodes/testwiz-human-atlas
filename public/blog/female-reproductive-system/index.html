Reproductive anatomy

Female reproductive system anatomy: uterus, ovaries, tubes, and clinical landmarks

A respectful, structured overview for medical, nursing, midwifery, and allied-health students.

Updated September 21, 2026 · 8 minute read · Educational reference

The female reproductive system includes organs that produce oocytes and hormones, provide a route for gamete transport, support pregnancy, and contribute to lactation after birth. Learn it as a connected pathway rather than as a memorised list: ovary → uterine tube → uterus → cervix → vagina.

Scope note: the Testwiz female HRA edition is a whole-body orientation surface with selected organs and incomplete skeletal and muscular coverage. It is useful for relationships and terminology, but it does not claim to show every structure or every human variation.

Ovary and uterine tube

Each ovary contains follicles at different stages of development. Hormonal signalling coordinates follicular growth, ovulation, and the ovarian cycle. The uterine tube has several named parts: the uterine or intramural part, isthmus, ampulla, and infundibulum with fimbriae.

The ovary is adjacent to, rather than continuous with, the tube. Fimbriae help guide a released oocyte toward the tubal opening. This spatial relationship is easier to understand by rotating a 3D pelvis than by looking at a single frontal diagram.

Uterus, cervix, and vagina

The uterus is a muscular hollow organ. The fundus is the rounded superior part, the body is the main central portion, and the cervix is the narrower inferior portion. The wall is commonly described as endometrium, myometrium, and perimetrium.

The cervix projects into the upper vagina. In clinical education, its location and changes are discussed alongside menstrual history, pregnancy, labour, screening, and infection prevention. Use course-specific guidance for examination technique and patient communication.

Mammary glands

Mammary glands lie in the subcutaneous tissue of the anterior chest wall. Lobes and ducts converge toward the nipple. The axillary tail and regional lymph nodes matter when learning breast examination and lymphatic drainage. The Testwiz atlas includes a selectable mammary gland reference in the female edition.

Nursing and midwifery links

Structure should support, not replace, clinical reasoning. Pelvic pain, abnormal bleeding, pregnancy findings, postpartum changes, breast symptoms, and infection concerns require a full history, examination, and local protocol. Use anatomy to explain where a symptom might be located and why a landmark matters; do not use an atlas to self-diagnose.

Quick check

What does the uterus do?

It receives the embryo, supports pregnancy, and its myometrium contracts during labour.

What is another name for the fallopian tube?

Uterine tube. “Fallopian tube” is the common clinical term.
